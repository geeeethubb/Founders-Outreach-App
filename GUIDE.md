# Outreach OS — User Guide
### For Founders: Illinois Entrepreneurs

> Your AI-powered relationship system for reaching YC founders, SF operators, mentors, investors, and recruiters. Built to make every cold email feel like a warm intro.

---

## What This App Does

Outreach OS replaces the painful "copy a template, edit 3 words, send, hope" cycle with a real research-and-personalization engine. You input a person, the AI studies them, finds what connects them to Illinois, and writes an email that references something specific about their work — not generic flattery.

**The core promise:** if someone reads your email and thinks "this person actually knows who I am," you will get a reply.

---

## The 5-Step Workflow

### Step 1 — Add a Contact
Go to **Contacts → Add Contact**. Fill in:
- Name + Company (required)
- Email address (required to send)
- LinkedIn URL (strongly recommended — improves research dramatically)
- Twitter handle, location, role
- Tags: use `YC Founder`, `Bay Area`, `Investor`, etc. to stay organized

**Pro tip:** Add 10–15 contacts at a time in a batch session rather than one-off. It's faster and lets you compare who to prioritize.

---

### Step 2 — Run AI Research
Open a contact's profile page and click **Run AI Research**.

The AI (GPT-4.1) will synthesize:
- A summary of who they are and why they're notable
- 3–5 specific talking points (recent launches, funding rounds, talks, writings)
- Shared context with UIUC / Illinois Entrepreneurs
- A relevance score (0–1) and suggested category
- A specific recommended ask

**This takes 15–45 seconds.** Don't skip it — emails generated without research are noticeably more generic.

#### Paste Mode (for best results)
For less well-known people (early-stage founders, local operators), the AI's training data may be thin. Use the 📋 button on the research card to paste their LinkedIn "About" section or bio. This dramatically improves hook quality.

**How to paste LinkedIn bio:**
1. Open their LinkedIn profile
2. Click "About" section → "See more"
3. Copy all the text
4. Paste into the research card text box
5. Click Run AI Research

---

### Step 3 — Compose Your Email
Go to **Compose**, select the contact, and choose your outreach goal:

| Goal | Use When |
|------|----------|
| 🎤 Speaker / Event | Inviting them to speak at an Illinois Entrepreneurs event or fireside |
| 🧭 Mentor / Advisor | Asking them to mentor a UIUC student founder for a semester |
| 💼 Internship / Jobs | Connecting your top students with their startup for internship or hire |
| 💰 Investor Intro | Requesting a meeting or intro for a student-led startup |

The AI generates **3 variants** — A, B, C — each opening with a different type of hook:
- **Variant A** hooks on a specific accomplishment (recent launch, funding, award)
- **Variant B** hooks on shared context (UIUC connection, Midwest roots, mutual interest)
- **Variant C** hooks on what's in it for them (talent pipeline, impact, network)

**Pick the variant whose hook feels most natural for this specific person.** Then edit freely — the AI gives you a strong starting point, not a final draft.

---

### Step 4 — Review, Edit, Send
Before hitting send, do a 30-second check:

- [ ] Is the subject line specific? (Not "Quick question" — something like "Illinois CS students → your internship pipeline")
- [ ] Does the first sentence reference something real and specific about them?
- [ ] Is the ask crystal clear and singular? (One ask, not three)
- [ ] Is it under 150 words? Shorter almost always wins
- [ ] Does it sound like a human wrote it, not a template?

Then click **Send**. The app routes through Resend, logs the send, and starts tracking.

---

### Step 5 — Track & Follow Up
Check **Conversations** for replies. The app classifies replies automatically:
- Positive → update the contact status to `replied` or `meeting`
- Neutral → consider a follow-up in 5–7 days
- No reply after 7 days → send one follow-up (see templates below)

**Log every outcome** on the contact's profile page — this data feeds the learning loop over time.

## Recommended Use Cases for Illinois Entrepreneurs

### Use Case 1: Speaker Series Outreach (Highest ROI)
**Goal:** Recruit 4–6 YC founders or SF operators to speak at semester events.

**Targeting criteria:**
- YC W20–S25 companies in B2B SaaS, AI, fintech, or consumer
- Founders who are active on Twitter/X (they're used to public engagement)
- People who have spoken at other universities or events before (check their LinkedIn)
- UIUC alumni first — they almost always say yes

**Workflow:**
1. Build a list of 20 targets (10 UIUC alumni, 10 YC founders in your space)
2. Run AI research on all 20 before writing a single email
3. Sort by relevance score — start with 0.8+
4. Use goal: **Speaker / Event**, add custom note: the event date and expected audience size
5. Send 3–4 per week max (quality > volume)

**What makes a great speaker ask:**
> "We're hosting 80 UIUC CS and engineering students on March 15th for our founder speaker series. Past speakers have included [name a notable one if you have one]. Would you be open to a 30-min Zoom fireside — our students would love to hear how you navigated [something specific from their story]."

---

### Use Case 2: Mentor Program Recruitment
**Goal:** Find 8–12 mentors for a semester-long student founder program.

**Targeting criteria:**
- Operators at Series A–C companies (they have knowledge + bandwidth)
- Domain experts matching your student founders' verticals
- People who explicitly mention "mentorship" or "giving back" in their LinkedIn bio
- Former YC founders who have exited or are now at larger companies

**Workflow:**
1. Map your student founders first — what domains/stages do they need help in?
2. Recruit by vertical match (fintech founder → fintech mentor)
3. Use goal: **Mentor / Advisor**, custom note: describe the specific student founder briefly
4. Lower the bar in the ask: "Just a 30-min intro call to see if there's a fit — no long-term commitment implied."

**Key insight:** Mentors say yes when they see a specific student who reminds them of themselves at that stage. Name the student, name what they're building, name the specific challenge. Generic "mentor our students" emails get ignored.

---

### Use Case 3: Internship & Jobs Pipeline
**Goal:** Secure internship offers for your top 20 members from Bay Area startups.

**Targeting criteria:**
- Engineering or product leads at 10–200 person startups
- Companies that have hired UIUC students before (check LinkedIn alumni data)
- YC companies in their growth phase (Series A–B, hiring aggressively)
- Founders who majored in CS/Engineering (peer-to-peer resonance)

**Workflow:**
1. Create a 1-page PDF of your "top members" — name, major, graduation year, GitHub, one project
2. Use goal: **Internship / Jobs**, custom note: mention the PDF and attach it separately
3. Personalize for their stack: "We have 3 students with strong React + Python backgrounds who specifically want to work on [their product area]."
4. The ask: offer to send the PDF OR schedule a 15-min call — give them a choice

**What to say to get a yes:**
> "UIUC CS students have gone on to Google, Jane Street, and Stripe — we have a few seniors this year who specifically want early-stage experience. Would it be helpful if I sent over a short doc on our top candidates?"

---

### Use Case 4: Investor Introductions for Student Founders
**Goal:** Get intro calls or warm intros for UIUC student-led startups.

**Targeting criteria:**
- Pre-seed / seed stage investors with a thesis that matches the startup
- Angels who are UIUC alumni (very high hit rate)
- Operators-turned-angels (more likely to take a punt on a student team)
- YC partners or group partners who run office hours (low-commitment first ask)

**Workflow:**
1. Write a tight 3-sentence description of the startup: what it does, traction if any, the ask
2. Use goal: **Investor Intro**, include the startup description in custom note
3. The ask should be modest: "a 20-min intro call" or "a warm intro to [specific investor name] if you think there's a fit"
4. Never ask for investment in the cold email — ask for the call

## Best Practices

### Personalization Rules
**Do:**
- Reference something they published, said, or built in the last 6 months
- Mention a specific product feature, funding round, or company milestone
- Use their name once in the first sentence — never more
- Make the connection between their work and your students feel obvious and natural

**Don't:**
- Start with "I hope this email finds you well"
- Say "I've been following your journey" (they know you haven't)
- Use phrases like "inspiring work," "thought leader," or "visionary"
- Send the same email to multiple people in the same organization
- CC people without permission

### Volume & Timing
- **Max 5–7 cold emails per day** — your sender reputation matters
- **Best days to send:** Tuesday–Thursday, 9–11am recipient's time zone
- **Avoid:** Monday mornings, Friday afternoons, and major tech event weeks (YC Demo Day, Thanksgiving)
- **Follow-up rule:** One follow-up only, 7 days later, 3 sentences max

### Subject Lines That Work
- "[Mutual name] suggested I reach out" (if true — never fake this)
- "Illinois CS students → your hiring pipeline"
- "Quick ask — speaking at UIUC's founder series"
- "Loved your [specific thing] — question for you"
- "UIUC student founder building [X] — intro?"

Subject lines that get ignored:
- "Quick question"
- "Collaboration opportunity"
- "Reaching out"
- "Coffee chat?"

### Follow-Up Template
If no reply after 7 days, send exactly this (edit lightly):

> Hi [Name] — bumping this up in case it got buried. Happy to keep it simple: [one-line restatement of ask]. Let me know either way — totally understand if the timing isn't right.
>
> [Your name]

That's it. Three sentences. No re-pitching.

The AI gets you 85% of the way there. The last 15% — the judgment call on tone, the specific edit that makes it sound like you, the decision to send or not — is yours. That 15% is what separates good outreach from great outreach.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Sending without reviewing.** The AI drafts are good but not always right. Always read before you send. The AI sometimes over-references something minor or misses a more obvious hook.

**Skipping research.** Emails generated without research are noticeably more generic. The 30-second research step is what makes the whole system work.

**Targeting too broadly.** "Any YC founder" is not a targeting strategy. The more specific your list — e.g., "YC W23/S23 founders in AI developer tools who went to Big Ten schools" — the higher your reply rate.

**Emailing the same person twice from different goals.** If you reach out to someone for speaker outreach and they don't reply, don't email them again 2 weeks later asking for a job referral. Pick one angle per person per semester.

**Over-following-up.** One follow-up. Never two. If they don't reply to the follow-up, move on. You can try again next semester.

## Quick Reference: Status Meanings

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `new` | Just added, not yet researched |
| `researching` | AI research in progress |
| `researched` | Research complete, ready to compose |
| `drafted` | Email written, not yet sent |
| `sent` | Email sent, waiting for reply |
| `replied` | They replied — follow up! |
| `meeting` | Call or meeting booked 🎉 |
| `archived` | Not pursuing right now |

## Semester Playbook

**Week 1–2:** Build your list. Add 30–40 contacts across your target categories. Run research on all of them. Sort by relevance score.

**Week 3–6:** Send outreach in batches. 5–7 emails per week. Focus on your top 20 first (relevance 0.8+). Log every reply and outcome.

**Week 7–8:** Follow up with non-replies. Start scheduling calls with positive replies. Update contact statuses.

**Week 9–12:** Run your calls. Log outcomes. Add any new contacts they refer you to.

**End of semester:** Archive contacts you're not pursuing. Export your meetings/outcomes. Use the data to improve your targeting for next semester.
